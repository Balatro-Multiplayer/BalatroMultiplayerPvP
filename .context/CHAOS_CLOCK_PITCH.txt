CHAOS CLOCK — DESIGN DIRECTION

Still head-to-head Balatro, but the run gradually becomes a little strange.
Not Cryptid-lite, exponential Mult, or a new ladder of overpowered rarities.
If Cryptid is Zalgo at maximum, this is Zalgo at minimum.
Familiar game, frayed edges, something slightly off.
Extraordinary things can happen; not everything becomes extraordinary.
Money, slots, hand choice, and your existing build should still matter.
Weirdness should change decisions, not just decorate the screen.
The question for an effect: what does this make you want differently?

# THE CLOCK

The Clock introduces rules and opportunities during the run.
A shop can behave differently, a blind can have a rule, an ante can have a theme.
Some effects end with their window; some explicitly last the rest of the run.
Temporary rules can leave permanent results: earned cards, levels, destruction.

Some encounters are forecast so you can decide whether to play or skip.
Others reveal when you enter; small incidents can arrive with the first hand.
Once revealed, the effect tells you what changed and how long it lasts.

Adding more effects should add variety, not make everything fire constantly.
Quiet stretches matter. The Clock doesn’t owe you a reward every blind.

A RUN BECOMES A DECK

One direction: don’t choose a deck up front; acquire its identity as you play.
Deck abilities become ingredients rather than locked starting packages.
Some can visit for an ante; others become part of the run.
Gradient is a good example: adjacent ranks also count for Joker effects.
That changes which Jokers you want without rewriting poker-hand recognition.

Starting gifts and deck transformations need different treatment from ongoing rules.
Early lasting effects give you time to build around them.
Later effects can reveal a new use for what you already own.
The accumulated combination matters more than each bonus in isolation.

THE KIND OF EFFECTS WE WANT

Higher Calling: Judgement produces unusually good Jokers this ante.
Now hunting Tarots or making room for a Joker becomes more tempting.

Cross-Pollination: Planets also level a random different hand you’ve played.
Now establishing a backup hand can have a future payoff.

Breakage Insurance: Glass breaks more often, but actual breakage pays.
For one blind it’s a cash-out opportunity; lasting, it could become an economy.

Negative Space: shop Jokers are unusually likely to be Negative.

These are candidate mechanics; numbers and durations still need testing.
The attraction is what you might build, not a guaranteed correct purchase.

MAYHEM, MYSTERY, AND TASTE

Calm offers occasional openings; Normal changes the situation more often.
Mayhem permits stranger combinations, lasting commitments, and some corruption.
It isn’t simply every number multiplied or every effect made permanent.

Not everything needs a downside; synergy and relief belong beside friction.
Mystery means what arrives next, what you get, and what works together.

It does not mean disguising Jokers or withholding their actual behavior.
Keep costs, restrictions, duration, and permanent consequences clear.

Write “unusually good Jokers,” not a spreadsheet of generation multipliers.
Exact tuning can change; changelogs can carry the numerical detail.
The Clock changes the situation. You decide what to make of it.
